## The leadership gap

A new prospective lead was introduced to a development team with the expectation that he would help guide the team’s work. His background was in accounts and business operations, not software delivery, and he had no prior working knowledge of Agile, Scrum, SDLC, Product Ownership, or the practical rhythm of managing a development team.

The challenge was not about commitment or capability. The main pain point was the gap between his existing accounts and business experience and the new responsibility of leading a software development team.

To support this transition, the training had to create a practical bridge. It needed to introduce the language of software delivery, the rhythm of Agile and Scrum, the responsibilities commonly expected from a Product Owner, and the basics of how work could be coordinated using the Microsoft 365 tool suite.

## Why Product Owner awareness mattered

Because the participant was coming from a business-facing background, the Product Owner role became an important part of the training. His existing accounts experience gave him a useful foundation for understanding value, priorities, stakeholder expectations, and business outcomes.

The training connected that foundation to how a Product Owner supports a development team: clarifying what needs to be built, helping organize and prioritize the backlog, translating business needs into workable items, and supporting acceptance conversations so the team understands what success looks like.

This was especially important because a non-technical lead can add value by strengthening clarity around the why, what, and priority of the work, even when the technical team owns the how.

> The Product Owner lens helped connect business understanding to delivery execution: value, priorities, backlog clarity, acceptance criteria, and stakeholder alignment.

## Why this training was needed

When someone from a non-technical background steps into a development leadership role, the first barrier is often not the technology itself. It is the unfamiliarity with how development teams think, plan, communicate, estimate, build, test, and release work.

The purpose of this training was to build enough understanding for the lead to work with the team more confidently. The focus was not to turn him into a developer, but to help him understand how development teams operate and how Product Owner responsibilities connect business value with team execution.

> The goal was leadership readiness, not technical depth: enough delivery and Product Owner understanding to guide conversations, support team rhythm, and create better visibility.

## Training focus

The learning was structured around five connected areas: Agile and Scrum, Product Owner responsibilities, SDLC, team collaboration, and Microsoft 365 tools for communication, visibility, and execution.

This helped frame development leadership as a system of clarity, cadence, collaboration, value alignment, and follow-through rather than as a purely technical responsibility.

## Outcome

By the end of the training, the participant had gained a working understanding of project management, Agile, Scrum, Product Owner responsibilities, and the basic structure of software development team operations.

Most importantly, the session helped reduce the initial knowledge gap and gave the participant a clearer starting point for leading and supporting a development team with stronger awareness of value, priorities, and delivery flow.
