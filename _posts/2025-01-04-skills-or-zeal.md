## The dream team question

When forming a dream team for a project, the checklist often starts with skills and knowledge. Those things matter. They are the ingredients that make the pizza possible.

But they are not the whole meal. A team also needs learning energy, willingness, ownership, and the right attitude to grow.

Over time, I have realized that the strongest teams are rarely built only by choosing the most technically impressive people. They are built by choosing people who can contribute, collaborate, learn, adapt, and bring energy into the work.

## When skill alone does not move the team

Someone can be technically strong and still difficult to work with. A person can have impressive expertise but no interest in collaboration, learning, or adapting.

That is like having a sports car with no engine. It may look impressive, but it does not move the team forward.

Skill creates the ability to contribute, but attitude often determines whether that contribution strengthens the team or creates friction.

## The fine line between harmony and chaos

Team dynamics need balance. Competition and motivation can energize people, but they can also create adverse effects when not grounded in trust and shared purpose.

The strongest teams are not built only around individual brilliance. They are built around how people learn, respond, collaborate, and move together.

Too little challenge can make a team passive. Too much ego can make a team chaotic. The right blend creates momentum.

## The selection process made it clear

Through screenings, interviews, and assessment activities, it became clear that skills and knowledge were important, but attitude and learning appetite made the difference.

Some candidates may not have checked every technical box, but they showed curiosity, humility, problem-solving energy, and the willingness to improve.

Those qualities matter because teams rarely work in perfectly predictable conditions. Projects change, expectations shift, and people need to learn their way through new situations.

## What I look for beyond the checklist

A good checklist helps evaluate technical fit, but it should not be the only lens. There are other signals that show whether someone can grow with a team and contribute to its rhythm.

For me, the strongest team members usually show a combination of capability, learning appetite, ownership, team energy, adaptability, and growth attitude.

## Skills are great and cool, but zeal is the fuel

Skills help people start. Zeal helps people continue learning, improving, and contributing when the work becomes difficult.

A team with skill but no energy can become rigid. A team with energy but no skill can become chaotic. The dream team needs both.

The real magic happens when knowledge, humility, curiosity, accountability, and enthusiasm come together.

## Closing reflection

Build the team with capability, but do not ignore the energy. Look for people who bring curiosity, positivity, determination, and the willingness to grow.

A strong team needs both skills and zeal.

The right people do not only complete tasks. They improve the quality of the room they are in.
